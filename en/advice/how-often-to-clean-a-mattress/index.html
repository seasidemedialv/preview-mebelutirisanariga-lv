How often to clean a mattress

How often to clean a mattress is not decided by one rule: the manufacturer and health sources we checked give no number that suits everyone. For everyday care, dry vacuuming, regularly washed bedding and airing are enough. A stain is treated at once, not on a schedule. Deep cleaning is needed when there is a stain, a smell or a dirty surface, and it should not become a routine, because moisture does a mattress no good.

What to do and when: a table

Situation What to do When
Normal use dry vacuuming, changing the bedding, airing regularly, as you usually do
Removable cover wash only as the label says when dirty, if the label allows
Spilt liquid blot with towels, clean only the stained spot at once
Urine, blood blot, cold water, no heat at once; stains on a mattress
Smell air it, find the cause when it appears; why a mattress smells
Mould do not clean it, deal with the moisture; the mattress usually has to be replaced at once; mould in a mattress
After illness or a change of tenants assess the condition when the user changes

The table sums up advice from manufacturers and health sources; it is not an official schedule. The “once a year” or “every six months” you see online cannot be found in the sources we checked, so we do not repeat it.

Why more frequent wet cleaning is not better

Moisture is a risk for a mattress. IKEA points out that foam is sensitive to moisture: too much water can damage the structure or leave moisture in the core. In a spring mattress, moisture can cause rust. Emma warns that excess moisture can increase the risk of mould. That is why the sources recommend treating stains locally and with as little liquid as possible.

TEMPUR is stricter still: its material must not be washed, sponge-cleaned or dry-cleaned, and the mattress must be protected from moisture. The cover has separate rules. So before any cleaning, read your manufacturer's care instructions and tell us what mattress you have.

Everyday care

  • Vacuum the mattress. Dry vacuuming suits delicate materials such as latex and cold foam.
  • Wash the bedding. If you are dealing with dust mites, Allergy UK recommends 60 °C or more.
  • Air the bedroom every morning for at least 15 minutes.
  • Keep humidity within the comfort range: 40–60%, and below 50% if you have mites.
  • With latex, avoid direct sun and heat.
  • Use a protective cover if you want the mattress to stay cleaner.

You do not need a service for this kind of care. Do not keep a coconut fibre mattress directly on the floor, because moisture can build up in it.

When it is worth ordering cleaning

  • there is a stain on the mattress that blotting has not removed;
  • the mattress has changed users and needs attention;
  • the surface looks dirty or grey;
  • there is a light smell that does not come from mould.

It is not a health measure: cleaning does not treat allergies and does not get rid of dust mites. More on those: dust mites in a mattress and sofa. After illness: mattress cleaning after illness.

When it matters to act at once

  1. Blot. Soak up as much liquid as you can with towels. Do not rub.
  2. Use cold water. For blood, use only cold water, and little of it. Hot water “cooks” the proteins, and a stain set by heat is hard to remove.
  3. Check how deep it went. If the liquid has gone through the cover, say so in the form. Whether cleaning makes sense depends on that.

Rental flat, hotel and hostel

There is no single norm here either, but there is a clear event: a change of user. At that point the mattress is inspected and a decision is made according to its condition. Businesses have a separate page: hotel and hostel mattress cleaning.

What may stay after cleaning

A stain may become less visible, but it may also stay, especially if it has been heated or washed with hot water. A urine smell may stay if the urine has reached the core. A mattress that has been soaked is a question of replacement, not cleaning. So a schedule alone solves nothing: acting at once matters more.

Frequently asked questions

Does a mattress have to be cleaned once a year?

The manufacturer and health sources we checked give no such number. If the mattress is clean, dry and free of smells, there is no need to order cleaning. Clean it when there is a reason.

Does a child's mattress need cleaning more often?

Accidents happen more often in a child's bed, and they need treating at once. More detail: child's urine on a mattress. There is no schedule here either, and we do not claim that cleaning improves a child's health.

Should both sides be cleaned?

Only if it is needed and if the mattress is made for use on both sides. The second side costs +50%.

Can I wash the cover whenever I like?

Only as far as the label allows. Many removable covers can go in a washing machine, but not all.
