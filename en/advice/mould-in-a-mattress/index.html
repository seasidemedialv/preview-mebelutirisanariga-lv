Mould in a mattress

Mould in a mattress usually means the mattress has to be replaced and the cause of the damp has to be found. Cleaning does not get mould out of foam, and we do not promise that it does. Inside a porous material mould can fill the empty spaces, and it may be hard or impossible to remove completely. The first job is not cleaning but stopping the damp in the room and under the mattress.

North Carolina State University material makes one more important point: if the inside of a mattress is not completely dry, mould comes back even after treatment. So a surface fix does not help in the long term.

Why mould appears in a mattress

Mould needs moisture. The Health Inspectorate says that relative air humidity above 80% in a room means a high risk of mould. Moisture can reach a mattress from several directions: from the air in the room, from a spilled liquid, from cleaning that is too wet or from where the mattress is placed.

  • The room: according to LSM, one in five buildings in Latvia has damp problems.
  • Liquid: foam is sensitive to moisture, and water can stay in the core.
  • Too much moisture during cleaning: excess moisture can raise the risk of mould.
  • Placement: a coconut fibre mattress on the floor can build up moisture.

Before you buy a new mattress, find the cause. Otherwise the new mattress will end up in the same conditions.

What to do now

  1. If possible, do not sleep on it. Especially if it is a child's mattress or the person sleeping on it has a respiratory or immune system condition.
  2. Measure the air humidity. Use a hygrometer to find out the humidity in the bedroom. The Health Inspectorate gives 40–60% as the comfort range, and up to 70% is cited as acceptable in a damp climate.
  3. Look over the room. Check the wall behind the bed, the corners and the base. If mould covers more than about 9 m² or is hidden in the building structure, the Health Inspectorate recommends professional help.
  4. Air the room. Open the bedroom window every morning for at least 15 minutes and, if possible, dry laundry outdoors.
  5. Do not douse the mattress. Do not pour water or any other liquid on the mattress to clean off the mould. That adds exactly what mould needs.

What cleaning cannot do and what it can

We do not guarantee that cleaning will eliminate mould, and we do not use such words. Cleaning is not disinfection and it is not a health measure. If you can see mould, there is usually no point in ordering cleaning.

But not every mark is mould. An old water or urine stain, a mark on the cover or a dirty patch can look similar. Send a photo: a specialist will tell you whether it is a job for mattress cleaning or whether it is more honest to replace the mattress.

How to protect the next mattress

  • Keep the air humidity within the comfort range. The US Environmental Protection Agency recommends below 60%, ideally 30–50%.
  • Air the bedroom every morning.
  • Blot spilled liquid straight away with towels; do not rub or soak it.
  • If the mattress is on the floor, check that the manufacturer allows it.
  • Wash a removable cover only as the label says.

The WHO stresses that the main way to avoid health effects is to eliminate damp and mould growth.

Frequently asked questions

Can mould be cleaned off if it is only on the surface?

The mark may disappear, but that does not prove the inside is clean. If the inside of the mattress is not completely dry, the mould comes back. That is why we promise nothing on this kind of job.

Can a mattress be dried in the sun or with a heater?

Latex should not be exposed to direct sun or heat. If a mattress has been soaked, it is almost impossible to dry before mould starts to grow, and sunlight will not solve that.

Is mould in a mattress dangerous to health?

The Health Inspectorate calls mould and damp significant risk factors for respiratory and immune system conditions. Ask a doctor about your own situation, especially if there are small children or people with asthma at home.

What should I do about a damp bedroom in a rental flat?

Measure the humidity, air the room and tell the landlord about mould on the walls. The source of the damp has to be dealt with in the room, not in the mattress.
