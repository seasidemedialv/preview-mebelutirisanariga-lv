Stains on a mattress

Stains on a mattress should be cleaned with a very small amount of liquid and only on the stain, because a mattress cannot be washed, and moisture in the core is a bigger problem than the stain. A fresh stain can usually be reduced. Yellow shadows, old stains and whatever has reached the core may stay.

Where liquid goes in a mattress

First aid

  1. Remove the bedding and cover Wash a removable cover only as its label says.
  2. Blot Use towels or paper to blot the liquid gently, without rubbing.
  3. Dampen only the stain Lightly dampen only the stained spot, not the whole mattress. Emma and IKEA both warn of the risk of excess moisture. Cool water, never hot.
  4. Leave it to dry Do not put bedding on while the spot is damp, and air the room.

Sources: TEMPUR UK, Emma, IKEA, Virginia Cooperative Extension. Checked 07.11.2026.

Stains by type

Stain First step What may stay
Blood Cold water, never hot: blood stain on a mattress A shadow set by heat
Urine Soak up with towels: urine smell in a mattress A smell from the core
Coffee, tea, wine Blot, cool water, do not soak Traces of colour
Yellow patches, unknown stain No experiments, send a photo Often partly stays

With yellow patches on an old mattress the cause often cannot be determined from a picture, so the outcome cannot be promised either. First tell us how old the mattress is and what has happened to it. Dark spots are a different matter, and they may be mould: mould in a mattress.

Sources: University of Georgia Extension (blood, wine, coffee), Emma. Checked 07.11.2026.

The filling decides how much moisture it can take

Filling What to bear in mind
Foam Too much water can damage the structure or leave moisture in the core
Springs Moisture can cause rust, as little liquid as possible
Latex, cold foam Dry vacuuming and a damp cloth, no strong substances
Coconut fibre Sensitive to moisture, do not place on a floor where moisture builds up
TEMPUR The manufacturer writes that the material cannot be washed, sponge-cleaned or dry-cleaned

We have not checked the rules for Dormeo and other brands, so follow your own manufacturer's instructions. Before you bring in a specialist, look at the manufacturer's care instructions: that is where it says what is allowed. If the mattress is orthopaedic, the label matters even more.

Sources: IKEA, TEMPUR UK, Kurlon. Checked 07.11.2026.

What to write on the form

  • The type and size of the mattress, if you know them.
  • How old the mattress is.
  • What the stain is and how old.
  • Whether the stain is on one side or both.
  • What has already been tried.

Attach photos of the stain and the label. They help a specialist give you a price and say whether it is worth cleaning both sides.

What not to do

  • Do not pour or spray a lot of liquid on the mattress.
  • Do not rub.
  • Do not use hot water, a hairdryer, a heater or an iron.
  • Do not use chlorine bleach or strong substances: they change the colour and can make latex brittle.
  • Do not put a damp coconut fibre mattress on the floor: moisture can build up there.

Home remedies vary from source to source, so we do not copy them. Keeping the core dry matters more than any remedy. Even if a remedy helps on the surface, excess moisture may stay inside.

What may stay and when a mattress needs replacing

Usually comes out

  • A fresh stain on the top layer
  • Light marks from everyday use
  • The cover, if the label allows washing

May stay

  • An old yellow shadow
  • A stain or smell that has reached the core
  • Dark mould spots
  • A stain set by heat

If there is mould in the mattress, or the smell does not go even after cleaning, the mattress needs replacing. NC State Extension writes that if the smell does not go after treatment, the mattress should be thrown away, because mould comes back if the inside is not completely dry. That refers to floods, but the logic is the same.

Source: NC State Extension (floods). Checked 07.11.2026.

Frequently asked questions

Can a stain be cleaned without wetting the mattress?

Dry vacuuming removes surface dust, but it usually does not remove a stain. You can try using very little moisture, on the stained spot only, and that is the closest thing to what manufacturers recommend.

Do both sides of the mattress need cleaning if the stain is on only one?

Not necessarily. The price is for one side, second side +50%. A specialist will tell you whether it is worth cleaning.

Can I just cover the stain with a mattress topper?

A topper does not remove a stain. If the spot is dry and has no smell, that is your choice. If it is damp, let it dry first, because moisture in a mattress raises the risk of mould.

Will the mattress be like new after cleaning?

No. Cleaning reduces stains and surface dirt, but it does not restore the mattress material and it does not remove what is in the core.
