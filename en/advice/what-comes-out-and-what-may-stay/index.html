What comes out and what may stay

What comes out and what may stay is decided by four things: how fresh the stain is, whether it has been heated, whether the liquid has reached the upholstery and what the fabric allows. Everyday dirt and fresh stains usually come out. Old stains, urine in foam, smoke smell and mould may stay partly or entirely. The specialist tells you this before work starts.

What happens under the fabric

The short answer: what usually works and what does not always

Usually comes out

  • Everyday dirt and dust on the top layer of the fabric
  • A fresh stain that has not been rubbed or heated
  • A wine or coffee stain, if it was blotted at once
  • Urine that has stayed in the fabric and has not soaked into the foam
  • A light smell, if its source is on the surface

May stay

  • An old stain, especially one exposed to a hairdryer, an iron or hot water
  • Urine and smell that have reached the foam or the mattress core
  • Smoke smell, which may come back
  • Mould in the upholstery
  • Water rings, colour changes and shrinkage from earlier washing
  • Wear, peeling faux leather and pressure marks on velour, because these are not stains

No stain is promised on this page. The words “usually” and “often” are here on purpose: the outcome depends on the particular stain and can only be judged from a photo or on the spot. We give no percentages, because two wine stains on two fabrics are not the same job.

Why some stains stay

Heat. A stain set by heat is hard to remove, and hot water can set a stain on upholstery too. In blood, hot water literally “cooks” the proteins. That is why only cold or cool water is right for a fresh stain.

Upholstery. Fabric can be cleaned, but foam holds moisture and cannot be dried quickly. That is why furniture and mattresses must not be soaked: too much water can damage the structure or leave moisture in the core. After a thorough soaking, it is almost impossible to dry a mattress before mould starts to grow.

Fabric. If fabric is overwetted, the finish moves and leaves a ring. When textiles get soaked, the colour often changes or bleeds, or the fabric shrinks. Water and pressure can flatten viscose pile for good.

Earlier attempts. Chlorine bleach and strong substances change the colour, solvents can harm the upholstery, and the wrong substance can make the colour run. If something has already been tried, say so in the form: it can change the outcome more than the stain itself.

Sources: University of Georgia Extension (blood, grease, wine), Emma (hot water and blood), Virginia Cooperative Extension (first aid), IKEA (foam and moisture), NC State Extension (soaked textiles and mattresses), Brentano Fabrics (pile). Checked 07.11.2026

What is not a stain and what cleaning does not fix

Some of what people want to “clean away” is a property of the material, or wear. Velour and chenille are pile fabrics on which pressure marks and shade changes can appear with use. Manufacturers state that this is a property of the fabric, not damage. Pilling is also typical of new natural-fibre fabrics.

The coating on faux leather can peel over time, and cleaning does not fix that. Natural leather absorbs liquid readily, stains soak into the leather itself, and even things that do textiles no harm can damage its structure. That is why leather and faux leather have their own assessment: leather sofa cleaning and faux leather sofa cleaning.

Sources: Long Eaton Sofas care guide (velour, chenille, natural fibres), Iowa State University Extension (leather). Checked 07.11.2026

What cleaning does not do

  • It does not treat allergies. Cleaning is not allergy treatment, and mites come back while the air stays damp.
  • It does not destroy mould in foam. Porous material that has gone mouldy may have to be thrown away, because mould can be hard or impossible to remove from it completely.
  • It does not remove bedbugs and fleas. That is a job for pest control. The Latvian register of service providers is on the website of the Health Inspectorate (Veselības inspekcija).
  • It does not replace dealing with the cause of the moisture. If the furniture gets wet again, the problem comes back too.

Sources: US EPA, A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home; Health Inspectorate; NHS, Bedbugs; UC IPM, Fleas. Checked 07.11.2026

The label decides what may be done

Furniture fabrics often have a code, usually found under the seat cushions or on the underside of the furniture. Codes are voluntary, so some furniture does not have one.

Code What it means
W Water-based foam, used sparingly, no overwetting, no dry-cleaning solvents
S Only a mild water-free solvent in a well-ventilated room; professional cleaning recommended
WS or S-W Either will do, in both cases test first
X No liquid at all, only vacuuming and light brushing; any other cleaning has to be decided separately

The letter W in a circle on a clothing care label means professional wet cleaning and does not correspond to furniture fabric code W. A cross through a symbol means that this treatment must not be used. Sources: Brentano Fabrics, Virginia Cooperative Extension, American Leather, NMSU G-402, GINETEX. Sources differ on code X. Frequently asked questions

Can you promise that my stain will disappear?

No. We can tell you what usually happens in such cases and what may stay. A promise about a stain we have not seen would not be honest, and on the spot the outcome may differ from what a photo suggests.

Is it worth paying if the stain may stay?

That is your decision, which is why we state the risks before work starts. If the whole sofa is cleaned, the benefit covers the rest of the surface too, not just one stain. If the question is about one old stain only, ask the specialist whether cleaning could change it at all.

What if my sofa has no label?

Codes are voluntary, so this happens. If there is no code, any cleaning is first tested in a hidden spot or checked with a professional. Send an overall photo and a close-up of the fabric.

Why do you not give percentages for how many stains come out?

Because we have no data to support them, and such a number cannot be applied to a particular stain. It matters more to know what may stay in your case.
