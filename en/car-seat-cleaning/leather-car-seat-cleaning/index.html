Leather does not tolerate much water, so the first step is to work out whether the seat is leather, faux leather or suede. One seat costs from €12, and the minimum order is €45. Send a photo, and the specialist will tell you the price and what to expect.

Is the seat leather, faux leather or suede

Material What to know
Finished leather Cared for with a soft cloth and a little mild soapy water; the suds are wiped off and the surface is buffed. Creams, saddle soap and oil do not suit finished leather.
Faux leather This is a coating on fabric, and over time it may peel. Cleaning does not repair it.
Suede This is the underside of the leather. Stains are better left to a specialist than rubbed, and if it gets wet, dry it in the air away from heat.

If you do not know the material, take a close-up photo of the seat's seam. If the owner's manual for the car has instructions on caring for the interior, follow them.

Why leather needs little water and nothing hot

Leather is very absorbent and stains soak into it, so methods that suit fabric can damage leather. Most leather suffers from direct contact with water, and saddle soap does not suit leather because it is alkaline. Heat dries it out, makes it brittle and distorts it. So seats must not be dried with a hairdryer or hot air, and in summer it is better to park the car in the shade.

After the work, air the car interior if the weather allows. The Canadian Conservation Institute notes that relative humidity above 65% encourages mould on leather, so it is better not to leave a damp car closed up.

More on the limits of leather care, which also apply to seats: can a leather sofa be cleaned. If leather furniture at home needs cleaning too, see the page on leather furniture cleaning.

What to do with a fresh stain on leather

Gently blot the liquid with a soft, clean cloth, without rubbing. Do not use solvents, chlorine bleach or hot water. Ink is an especially hard case: nobody can promise it will come off leather, so do not rub it: send a photo instead. Coffee or juice spilt in the car is best blotted straight away, before the stain soaks into the seam.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • surface dirt and dust in the seams
  • fresher drink stains
  • a layer of everyday dirt on the seat surface

May stay

  • ink on leather
  • worn colour and cracks
  • peeling faux leather coating
  • a stain that has soaked into the leather
  • a smell from the upholstery

A worn seat edge by the door is wear, not dirt. Leather repair and recolouring are a different job, and we do not do them. From a photo, the specialist will tell you whether the stain is on the surface or already in the leather and whether cleaning makes sense.

Frequently asked questions

Does leather need cream after cleaning?

According to a US university's care recommendations, creams, saddle soap and oil do not suit finished leather. If the car's manual says otherwise, follow it.

Do I need to bring the car somewhere?

No, the work is done at your place in Riga or the Riga region. In the form, say where the car will be parked and whether there is access there.

The seat smells. Will leather cleaning solve that?

Not always. If the smell comes from the upholstery, surface cleaning may reduce it, but it may stay or come back. Smell treatment is extra, from €15, and the specialist will tell you whether it is worth ordering.

Do you also clean the roof lining, floor and whole interior?

No. We clean seats, not the whole interior. If you need a full interior clean, car interior specialists do that.
