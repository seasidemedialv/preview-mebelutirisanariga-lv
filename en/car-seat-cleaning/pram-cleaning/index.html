What can and cannot be cleaned on a pram

Part What to expect
Carrycot and seat Cleaned according to the label or manual.
Hood and footmuff Cleaned according to the label. If the fabric has a waterproof coating, show it in a photo.
Carrycot mattress Treated as a small mattress: less liquid and careful drying.
Straps and buckle Only what the pram's manual allows.
Frame, wheels, brakes, folding mechanism This is not cleaning. Mechanical problems are a matter for a pram repair service.

What the label and manual say

Pram manufacturers give care instructions on the label of the removable cover and in the manual. These instructions are decisive, because prams differ and we have no verified general rules for all models. A photo of the label and the model name tell us more than any general advice.

There is often padding under a pram's fabric. Too much water may leave rings, make colours run, shrink the fabric and wet the padding. So the fabric must not be soaked. After cleaning, leave the pram open in a ventilated room, and do not fold it while the fabric is damp. Excess moisture may encourage mould.

Pram dry cleaning: what the term means

Pram dry cleaning in everyday speech usually means any cleaning by a specialist, not literal dry cleaning at a dry cleaner's. If the circle on the label is crossed out, this treatment must not be used. So it is the label, not the term, that tells you what suits the pram.

What to do with a fresh stain

Gently blot it with a clean cloth, without rubbing. If needed, dampen the cloth slightly in cool water, and do not wet the fabric through. Do not use hot water or chlorine bleach. If the stain is small and fresh, that is often enough.

What to prepare before cleaning

  • Take photos of the pram, the stain and the label on the fabric.
  • Enter the model in the form and which parts are removable.
  • Tell us what was spilt and how long ago.
  • Choose a place where the pram can stand open and dry after the work.

What to expect from cleaning

Usually comes out

  • crumbs, sand and dust in the fabric
  • fresher stains on the cover
  • some of the smell from the fabric

May stay

  • black mould spots inside the fabric
  • an old milk or urine stain
  • fabric faded by the sun
  • a smell in the padding

Black spots on the hood or carrycot of a pram that has been kept for a long time in a cellar or garage may be mould. In porous material, mould may be hard or impossible to remove completely, and then the question becomes replacing that fabric part. A close-up photo will help show whether cleaning makes sense. On the limits of cleaning: what comes out and what may stay.

For a used pram bought from another owner, cleaning is a sensible start, but before use also check the brakes, buckles and straps according to the manual. We do not check these parts.

Frequently asked questions

Does the pram need to be taken apart before cleaning?

Remove what the manual allows to be removed without tools, and take a photo of how the parts were assembled. If something will not come off, leave it. In the form, write what is removable.

Can you repair the pram's mechanism or wheels?

No, a pram repair service does that. We clean the fabric.

How should I store a pram so it does not start to smell?

Completely dry, in a ventilated place, not in a damp cellar. The Health Inspectorate (Veselības inspekcija) states that a room with relative humidity above 80% has a high risk of mould.

When can the pram be used again?

When the fabric and padding are completely dry. We do not promise a time, because it depends on air humidity, ventilation and the fabric.
