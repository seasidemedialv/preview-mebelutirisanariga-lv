Why a salon is a separate job

Salon furniture does not get dirty just from being sat on. Cosmetics, hair dye, oils and creams get onto it, and many chairs and sofas are faux leather or light fabric, which looks good in a photo but quickly shows smudges.

A salon is usually small: one waiting area and a few workstations. So the volume is not large, and timing matters more than price. Every chair that cannot be used is a client not seen, so it is better to plan the cleaning for your closed day.

  • Waiting sofa and pouffes: usually what clients see most.
  • Hairdressing chairs: often with a faux leather cover and a complicated shape.
  • Manicure and pedicure chairs: check whether the upholstery is fabric or a wipe-clean cover.
  • Massage tables and daybeds: see daybed cleaning and tell us what the cover is.

Hair dye, nail polish, oil

We have no general rule for hair dye and nail polish, as these are very different products, and the result depends on the fabric. Such stains may stay. So we do not promise they will disappear, and the specialist will be able to give the best answer from the photos.

These rules matter for staff: do not rub the stain and do not pour acetone or other solvents on it. Solvents can damage upholstery foam and colour, and on faux leather they can damage the coating. Blot liquid gently with a clean cloth, without rubbing, and send a photo. Blot oil and cream the same way.

A faux leather coating may peel over time, and cleaning cannot repair that. If a chair's armrests are already peeling, cleaning will not restore them.

What to put in the form

  • A furniture list: chairs, sofas, pouffes, work chairs.
  • Photos: the furniture, the material, close-ups of stains, the label.
  • What the stain is, if you know: dye, polish, oil, cream.
  • When the salon is closed or how many workstations can go unused.
  • Address, floor and entrance.

Hygiene and rules

Furniture cleaning is not disinfection, and it does not replace the salon's hygiene procedure. Treating tools and work surfaces stays the salon's responsibility. If the salon needs documents or regular cleaning, ask the specialist.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • Everyday dirt on the waiting sofa
  • Smudges on light fabric
  • Fresh oil marks, if blotted up straight away

May stay

  • Hair dye
  • Nail polish
  • Oil that has soaked into the foam
  • Peeling faux leather coating

Tell us what was spilt, when it happened and whether anyone has tried to clean the stain. This helps the specialist understand what to expect, and the advice on whether cleaning makes sense depends on it too. More: stains on a sofa.

Frequently asked questions

Can you remove a hair dye stain from a light sofa?

We do not promise that. The result depends on the fabric and the dye. Send a photo and tell us how old the stain is.

Do you clean chairs between two clients?

No, that is the salon's daily cleaning. We clean upholstery as a separate job and agree the time in advance.

Is it worth cleaning a faux leather chair?

Yes, if the coating is intact. If it is peeling, cleaning will not restore the look, and it is better to replace or reupholster the chair. We do not do reupholstery.

We have one small sofa. Will you come just for that?

Yes. The minimum order is €45: if the total is lower, you pay €45. A two-seater sofa from €35 is below that amount, so it is worth adding pouffes or chairs that you want cleaned anyway.
