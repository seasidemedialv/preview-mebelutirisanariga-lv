The seat stays in the row

Each cinema seat folds up and has an upholstered back and often a cup holder. It is bolted to the floor, so the work happens in the narrow space between rows. This affects access and ventilation, so the job differs from cleaning conference chairs, which can be moved.

Stains are hard to see on dark fabric, so take photos with the lights on in the hall.

  • Seat: popcorn grease, sugar and drinks.
  • Back: dirt from hair and hands, especially along the top edge.
  • Gaps between seat and back: crumbs and hard dirt.
  • Armrests and cup holders: often plastic, which is not upholstery.

Popcorn grease and sugary drinks

A grease stain soaks into the fabric, and solvent can damage the upholstery foam, so do not pour anything on the stain. A sugary drink leaves a sticky layer as it dries, and stains are hard to see in dark fabric.

If a drink has been spilt in a row, staff can blot it gently with a clean cloth before the next showing, without rubbing. Do not use hot water. Write down the seat number so that it can be looked at.

Why a photo of one row matters more than a list

Seats in one hall are usually the same, but their condition is not: the front rows are used less often, and the rows in the middle of the hall and near the entrance the most often. So photograph one row in the middle, one at the side and the worst seats. Then the specialist can assess the whole hall without looking at each seat, and you do not have to send a hundred photos.

The hall schedule

In a cinema a hall is usually free only outside showing times, and you know best when it can be closed. If the fabric is damp after the work, the hall is not yet ready for the audience. Drying depends on air humidity, temperature, ventilation, the fabric and the filling, and the seat foam may be thick.

So we do not promise a deadline for all halls at once. It is more realistic to plan one hall at a time or to divide the work by rows, leaving some rows unsold. Agree this with the specialist. The same goes for theatre and concert hall seats.

What to put in the form

  • The number of halls and the number of seats in each.
  • Photos: one row, a close-up of one seat, the worst spots, the label or manufacturer's description.
  • When the hall is free and how many rows can be left unsold.
  • Material: fabric, velour, faux leather.
  • Whether the ventilation can be run and whether the hall can be aired.

What to expect after cleaning

Usually comes out

  • Dust and crumbs in gaps
  • An everyday greyish look on seats
  • Fresh drink stains

May stay

  • Old popcorn grease that has soaked into the foam
  • Sticky sugary drink stains that have dried deep in
  • Pressure marks on velour
  • Wear on armrests

On velour, pressure marks and differences in shade are a property of the fabric, and cleaning does not remove them. More: grease stain on a sofa.

Frequently asked questions

Can you clean a hall between showings?

Usually not, as there is too little time between showings and the fabric must dry. If you have a longer break, tell us.

Do the seats need to be dismantled before work starts?

Usually not, as the seats stay in the row. The photos will show whether any row is hard to reach.

Do you also clean theatre or concert hall seats?

Yes, if the seats have soft upholstery. For velour and expensive fabrics, the label or the manufacturer's instructions must be checked first.

Will the seats be available for sale that evening after cleaning?

We do not promise that. Allow a margin and agree it with the specialist.
