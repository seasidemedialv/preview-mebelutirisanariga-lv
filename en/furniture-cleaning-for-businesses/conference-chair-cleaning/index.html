Many matching chairs are a different job

In an office each chair has its own history, but in a conference hall a hundred chairs are the same model and usually equally worn. So you do not need to photograph each one: one typical chair, the worst chair and the label are enough.

The same fabric also means the result has to be judged as a whole. A clean chair beside an uncleaned one may look lighter, so it is better to clean the whole hall rather than just the front row. If the budget does not allow that, agree with the specialist which part will be less visible.

  • Chairs in rows: they stay in the hall, and there must be space between the rows for access.
  • Stacking chairs: these can be set out separately, but they need somewhere to stand.
  • Chairs with a writing tablet: the tablet is not fabric, so tell us if you have them.
  • Meeting rooms: fewer chairs, but coffee stains more often.

Plan around the hall's calendar

A conference hall lives by its bookings, so the key thing is to choose a free slot between events. Do not plan the work for the day before a big conference: if the fabric is damp, the chairs cannot be used yet, and drying depends on air humidity, temperature, ventilation, the fabric and the filling, not on a promise.

In a large hall the air circulates less well if the ventilation is off. Tell us whether the ventilation can be run and whether windows can be opened. While the chairs are drying, do not stack them, as fabric dries more slowly in a stack.

If the whole hall cannot be cleared at once, agree on cleaning it in parts. The specialist will say whether that is sensible.

What to put in the form

  • The number of chairs in each hall and whether they all match.
  • Photos: one chair from the front and from behind, the worst chair, the label under the seat.
  • Availability: the dates when the hall is free, and the next event.
  • Whether the chairs can be moved and where they can be put.
  • Entrance, floor, lift, access outside events.

The label and the fabric

If the chairs were bought in one batch, the label on one chair usually applies to all of them. Code W means water-based foam with little moisture, S a water-free solvent, WS both, and X only vacuuming and light brushing. The codes are voluntary. If there is no code, the fabric must be tested on a hidden spot before cleaning.

On velour chairs, pressure marks and differences in shade are a property of the fabric. They may stay after cleaning.

What the hall can expect

Usually comes out

  • An even greyish look on seats and backs
  • Dust in seams
  • Fresh coffee stains

May stay

  • Old coffee and ink stains
  • Pressure marks on velour
  • Wear on the edge of the back, where chairs are lifted
  • Smell, if the liquid has reached the foam

In a row of a hundred chairs, an old stain on one chair may stay, but the hall as a whole usually looks more even. The specialist will tell you from the photos what to expect.

Frequently asked questions

Can you clean 200 chairs in one go?

We cannot promise that without an inspection. Tell us the number and when the hall is free, and the specialist will say whether the work must be divided into several visits.

Do the chairs need to be stacked or laid out before work starts?

Leave them as agreed with the specialist. If they are stacked in storage, show this in a photo so that the space can be planned.

What if the hall has several types of chair?

List each type separately and add photos. The price is set per unit, and leather or faux leather chairs should be listed separately.

Can we get a quote before deciding?

Yes. From the photos and the number of chairs, the specialist will give you the price.
