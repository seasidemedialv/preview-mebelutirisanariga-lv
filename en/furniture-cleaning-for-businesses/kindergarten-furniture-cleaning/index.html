How a kindergarten differs from a home

At home a sofa is used a few times a day, but in a kindergarten one group uses it all day, and the furniture is right at child height. Many of the pieces are small, but there are more of them, and they are spread across the groups.

A kindergarten also has its own routine. Children are in the rooms from morning to evening, so tell us in the form when the groups are out of the room, for example in summer or when a group is closed.

  • Sofas and small chairs in the play corner: food, pencils and liquids.
  • Pouffes and soft play modules: there is no general rule for wipe-clean covers; the label decides.
  • Nap mattresses: first we need to see whether there is a removable cover.
  • Resource room and head's office: office chairs.

Urine: what may stay

If urine is on the surface and fresh, the stain can often be reduced. If it has reached the foam, the smell may stay or come back. How far it has gone cannot be known from the outside, so we give no promise. More: child urine on a mattress.

The most important thing for the teacher is to blot the moisture gently with a clean cloth straight away, without rubbing, and not to use hot water. It is often better value to replace a mattress that has been soaked several times.

What we do not promise

Kindergartens often ask whether furniture is completely safe after cleaning. We make no claims about that. Cleaning is not disinfection, does not treat allergy and does not promise that the fabric will be free of dust mites. On mites, sources mention air humidity, frequent vacuuming and washing bedding at 60 °C, not a one-off furniture clean. More: dust mites in a mattress and sofa.

The kindergarten's hygiene procedure is set by the institution itself. If you have specific requirements, add them to your request and ask the specialist whether they can be met.

What the kindergarten can do before work starts

Take toys, cushions and blankets off the sofas and pouffes. Take bedding and covers off the nap mattresses, if they are removable and meant to be washed according to the label. Make sure the room can be aired, as moving air helps the fabric dry, and plan for the group to return only when the fabric is dry.

What to put in the form

  • Furniture per group: sofas, pouffes, small chairs, nap mattresses.
  • Photos: each type, the label, stains, the mattress cover.
  • When the groups are out of the room and how many rooms can be cleared at a time.
  • Whether there are urine stains and how old they are.
  • Address, entrance and the staff member in charge.

What to expect after cleaning

Usually comes out

  • Everyday dirt on upholstered furniture
  • Fresh food and juice stains
  • Dust in seams and gaps

May stay

  • Urine smell, if the liquid has reached the foam
  • Old juice stains
  • Pencil and felt-tip marks
  • Torn or cracked covers

If a mattress or pouffe still smells after cleaning, it usually needs replacing: the cause is in the filling, and cleaning again may not solve it.

Frequently asked questions

Is the furniture safe for children after cleaning?

We make no such claim. Ask the specialist about what matters to you and, if needed, check the institution's requirements.

Can you clean in a room where children are present?

Better not: the fabric must dry before the furniture is used again. Tell us in the form when the groups are out of the room.

Is it worth cleaning a pouffe with a tear?

If the filling is exposed, cleaning will not repair it, and moisture may get into the filling. Send a photo and the specialist will say whether it is worth it.

Will several visits be needed if there are several groups?

That depends on the volume and on access to the rooms. The specialist will tell you from the list.
