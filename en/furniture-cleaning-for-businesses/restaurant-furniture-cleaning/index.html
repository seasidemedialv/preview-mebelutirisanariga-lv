How a restaurant is different

An upholstered wall bench usually cannot be moved, so it stays in the dining room. The space between seat and back is a pocket where crumbs collect, and the edge by the table gets soiled by hands and clothes. A seat near the entrance wears out faster than one in a far corner.

The second difference is the type of dirt. On an office chair the dirt is dust and hand marks; in a restaurant it is grease, sauces, wine, coffee and sugar. Do not pour solvent on a grease stain, as it can damage the upholstery foam.

  • Upholstered benches and built-in sofas: state the length, how the seats are divided and whether the cushions are removable.
  • Chairs with an upholstered seat: often the same model, so easy to count.
  • Terrace cushions: these are a different job, see outdoor cushion cleaning.
  • Private rooms and VIP rooms: lighter fabrics, velour, faux leather.

Faux leather, fabric, velour

If the seats are faux leather, bear in mind that the coating may peel over time, and cleaning cannot repair that. Before you send a request, look over the seams and the places people sit on most often, and photograph any cracks.

For fabric, the label sets the cleaning method, if there is one. On velour, pressure marks are a property of the fabric, so they may stay after cleaning.

What staff should do with a fresh stain

  1. Blot Use a clean cloth or paper towel to blot gently, without rubbing.
  2. Cool water Dampen a wine or coffee stain lightly with cool water, without soaking the fabric. Do not use hot water.
  3. Pour nothing else on it Bleaches and strong household products can change the colour of the fabric. Leave grease to the specialist.
  4. Write it down Note what was spilt and when. This helps later, when you have to decide what to do about the stain.

What to put in the form

  • A list of seating: how many chairs and sofas and how many metres of bench.
  • Photos: the dining room as a whole, the bench seams, stains, the label if you can find it.
  • When the dining room is empty: the closed day, the hours before opening or after closing.
  • How many seats can go unused at a time if you cannot close fully.
  • Address, delivery entrance, parking.

What a restaurant can expect

Usually comes out

  • Crumbs and dirt in seams
  • An everyday greyish look on seats
  • Fresh wine and coffee stains

May stay

  • Old grease stains that have soaked into the foam
  • Food smell in the upholstery
  • Peeling faux leather
  • Fabric worn to a shine

Kitchen and frying smells also come from the air in the room, and furniture cleaning does not solve that. We work with upholstery, not with the room. More: what may stay after cleaning.

Frequently asked questions

Can you clean after closing time?

We do not promise that in advance. Tell us your closing time and when the dining room must be ready. The specialist will say whether that time is possible and agree it with you.

Can the dining room open the same day?

That depends on how damp the fabric will be and on the air humidity in the room. Allow a margin and ask the specialist.

Do all the seats have to be cleaned at once?

No. You can start with the worst spots. But next to a cleaned seat the others may look darker, so it is better to clean a bench of one fabric along its whole length.

How can I tell faux leather from leather?

Look at the label or the purchase documents. If you are not sure, take a close-up of the surface and the seams, and the specialist will try to identify it.
