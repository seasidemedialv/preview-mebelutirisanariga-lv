What is really our work in a school

Most school chairs are wood or plastic, and they are looked after in routine cleaning. Our work starts where there is fabric, upholstery or faux leather.

  • Assembly hall: many matching chairs, often in rows. Read more about this kind of work on the page conference chair cleaning.
  • Staff room: sofas and chairs with coffee stains.
  • Library and reading room: pouffes and upholstered chairs.
  • Head's office and school office: office chairs.
  • Lounge areas in corridors: soft seating modules and sofas used by all pupils.

If a pouffe or module has a PVC or other wipe-clean cover, we have no general rule: check the manufacturer's instructions and add a photo.

The easiest time is the school holidays

In a school the hardest thing is not the fabric but the calendar. The rooms are busy from morning to evening, and the assembly hall may be booked for clubs and events. In the holidays everything is simpler, as the rooms are empty and the furniture can dry undisturbed.

Allow a margin before the first day of school or graduation. Drying depends on air humidity, ventilation, temperature, the fabric and the filling, so we do not promise a number of hours. The Health Inspectorate (Veselības inspekcija) gives 40–60% as comfortable indoor air humidity.

Ink and felt-tip pens: what may stay

In a school, stains come from pens and felt-tips. Ink on fabric is a difficult stain, and on leather it may prove impossible to remove. Such stains may stay. Do not pour solvents on them: they can damage upholstery foam.

Scratches and tears are not dirt, and cleaning will not repair them. A chair with a sagging seat and a worn back will still look old after cleaning. Ask the specialist whether that chair is worth cleaning.

If the budget does not allow everything at once

A school often has to choose what to do first. Start with the rooms most people see: the assembly hall and lounge areas. The staff room sofa and library chairs can be the next stage. If you split the list by room and add photos, the specialist can give a price for each part separately, and the school decides where to start.

What to put in the form

  • A list of chairs and other furniture by room.
  • Photos: one typical chair, the worst chair, the label if you can find it.
  • The school holidays or the days when the rooms are free.
  • How to get into the school outside teaching hours: entrance and the staff member in charge.
  • Whether the price is needed for a quote or a tender.

What a school can expect

Usually comes out

  • A greyish look on upholstered chairs
  • Dust and everyday dirt
  • Fresh drink stains

May stay

  • Pen and felt-tip ink
  • Old stains cleaned earlier with unknown substances
  • Scratches and tears
  • Wear on the edges of assembly hall chairs

We do not promise that furniture will be suitable for allergic children after cleaning, and we do not replace the school's routine cleaning and hygiene procedure.

Frequently asked questions

Do you also clean wooden or plastic chairs?

No. That is routine cleaning work. We clean soft upholstery.

The school needs a price for a tender. Can you send a quote?

Say so in the form and add the list and photos. The specialist will give you the price.

Can you clean while lessons are going on?

Better in an empty room, as the fabric must dry. Tell us which rooms are free and when, and agree it with the specialist.

Can you work before the school year starts?

It depends on the volume. Send the dates in good time, and the specialist will say whether it is possible.
