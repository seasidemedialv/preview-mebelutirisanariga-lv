What a children's sofa goes through that others do not

On an adult's sofa the biggest problem is usually one worn seat. A children's sofa gets a little of everything: food crumbs in the gaps, liquids that soak in, coloured marks and night-time accidents if a child sleeps on it. There are many stains, they vary, and parents often do not know what they were.

So before cleaning it helps to write down what the child does on the sofa: sleeps, eats, draws. That says more than a single photograph, and a specialist can assess what is a stain here and what is wear.

Felt-tip, pencil and juice

Ink is one of the hardest stains. The result depends on the pen and the fabric, and red ink calls for a separate approach. Do not rub it with a wet cloth, because the ink spreads. Send a close-up photo and write what kind of felt-tip it was, if you know.

For juice and other drinks, the first step is to blot with a clean cloth, not to rub and not to use hot water. If a lot of liquid was spilt, look beyond the fabric, because the liquid can go through to the upholstery.

Urine on a child's sofa

An accident on the sofa is a common reason for a request. Blot up as much liquid as you can, do not rub and do not use hot water. If the urine has gone through the fabric to the foam, the smell may stay even after cleaning. More detail: cleaning urine from a sofa. If there is a mattress instead of a sofa: child's urine on a mattress.

Allergies, mites and what we cannot promise

If a child has an allergy, cleaning is not treatment. Sources do not confirm that one upholstery cleaning removes mites or improves symptoms. Mites breed in upholstered furniture as long as a room is damp, so what matters more is air humidity below the recommended limit, regular vacuuming and washing bedding at 60 °C or hotter. Ask a doctor about this, and read more on the page dust mites in a mattress and sofa.

If a child has sensitive skin or you have questions about what will be used, write it in the form so that the specialist knows before the work. Ventilate the room, and let the child sit on the sofa only when the fabric is completely dry.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • crumbs and dust in the gaps;
  • fresh juice and food stains;
  • greyness on the seat.

May stay

  • felt-tip or ink marks, especially red;
  • urine smell, if it is in the foam;
  • stains rubbed with an unknown liquid;
  • torn fabric and wear.

Frequently asked questions

Will a cleaned sofa be suitable for a small child?

We do not make that promise. If a child has an allergy or sensitive skin, ask a doctor: you do not need to give health details in the form. Let the child back on the sofa only when it has dried.

Does plasticine come out of a sofa?

We have no verified source on plasticine, so we promise nothing. Do not rub it or heat it. Send a photo: a specialist will tell you what to expect.

How often should a children's sofa be cleaned?

There is no set interval. Request cleaning when you see stains or notice a smell. If the aim is to deal with mites or allergies, how often you clean is not the right tool: air humidity and vacuuming matter more.

When is it better to clean: now or when the child has grown up?

A fresh stain is usually easier to clean than an old one, so a large stain is not worth putting off. If the dirt is minor, cleaning can wait and be ordered together with another job.
