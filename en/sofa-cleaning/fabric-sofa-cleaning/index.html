What the codes on the care label mean

Code What it calls for
W water-based cleaning foam, sparingly, without soaking; no chemical solvents
S only a mild water-free solvent in a well-ventilated room, with a test first; professional cleaning recommended
WS or S-W a mild solvent or mild washing foam, with a test in either case
X no liquids at all, only vacuuming and light brushing; any other cleaning is assessed separately

The label is usually hidden under the cushions or on the underside of the sofa. The codes are not compulsory, so some sofas do not have one. The fabric then needs a test in a hidden spot or a specialist's assessment before any cleaning.

The furniture codes W, S, WS and X do not belong to the same system as clothing care symbols. The letter W in a circle (professional wet cleaning) is not the furniture code W. Sources: Brentano Fabrics, Virginia Cooperative Extension, GINETEX; checked 07.11.2026.

Why too much water is the main risk

Where it gets too wet, the fabric's finish can migrate and leave a ring that shows even after drying. Wet fabric can shrink, and the colour can bleed. If liquid passes through the fabric it reaches the upholstery, and drying it out from there is much harder.

So the right question is not how hard to clean but how little moisture is enough for this particular fabric and stain. The difference between dry and wet cleaning is explained on the page dry or wet furniture cleaning.

Cotton, linen and chenille

Some natural fibres can change size with moisture, so the label matters especially on a cotton or linen sofa. On linen, sources disagree: some allow water, others solvent. There is no one rule for all: the code decides.

Pilling on new natural-fibre fabric is normal, not dirt. On chenille and velour, pressure marks and shade changes arise with use, and they are not damage. For those, see the separate page velour sofa cleaning. Microfibre and flock have no verified rules of their own: microfibre and flock sofa cleaning.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • greyness and everyday dirt;
  • fresh coffee, tea and food stains;
  • dust and crumbs under the cushions.

May stay

  • an old stain that has set in the fabric, especially after hot water or ironing;
  • a water ring from earlier cleaning;
  • faded colour and wear;
  • a smell that comes from the upholstery.

Frequently asked questions

What if there is no label or it has faded?

Send a close-up photo of the fabric and say where and when the sofa was bought. The codes are voluntary, so this is nothing unusual. Fabric without a code needs a test in a hidden spot before any cleaning.

What if the code is X?

X means liquids must not be used: that leaves vacuuming and light brushing. Sources disagree about the rest. So we assess such a sofa separately and may advise against cleaning it.

Can I wash the cover in a washing machine if it has a zip?

A zip does not mean the cover may be washed. It can shrink, damage the backing or change colour. More detail: sofa cushion cleaning.

Can a ring appear on the fabric after cleaning?

It can, if the fabric got too wet or already has an unstable finish. That is exactly why the code and the amount of moisture matter. Honestly, no one can promise it will never happen.
