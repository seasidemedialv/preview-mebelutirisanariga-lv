Why there are no ready-made recipes on this page

Microfibre and flock are trade names for a broad group of fabrics, so the name alone cannot tell you what a particular sofa will tolerate. That is set by the care label and the condition of the fabric itself.

We do not want to repeat claims we cannot check. Various fabrics are sold as “microfibre” and “flock”, so a rule that suits one sofa may not suit another. What decides it is the manufacturer's code on the sofa in question.

What certainly applies to these fabrics too

Some rules are general and do not depend on the type of fibre:

  • The code on the label decides whether water, solvent or neither may be used. The explanation is on the page fabric sofa cleaning.
  • If there is no code, the fabric needs a test in a hidden spot before cleaning.
  • Too much water can leave a ring and wet the upholstery.
  • Blot a fresh stain, do not rub, and do not use hot water.
  • Chlorine bleach and strong cleaning solutions can change the colour.

If the sofa was bought without a label, the purchase description, the shop's product code or the manufacturer's name will also do. With these you can find the care instructions. If the sofa was bought in a local shop, you can often ask there what the label said.

What to send before your request

  1. Label Look under the cushions and on the underside of the sofa. Photograph it so that the code is legible.
  2. Close-up of the fabric In daylight, with a coin in the shot to show the scale of the fabric.
  3. The stain and the whole seat Separately, the stain up close and the whole seat, to show whether it is a stain or fabric wear.
  4. What has been used Write whether the spot has already been cleaned and with what, if you remember.

From that, a specialist can say whether the information is enough or an on-site inspection is needed. Other tips on the shots: how to photograph a sofa for a price.

What is the honest answer for such a sofa

If the code is W or WS and the fabric is undamaged, the work is usually much like that on any other fabric sofa. If the code is S or X, or there is no label at all, a specialist may advise cleaning less or not cleaning. Such advice is not a refusal but a way of not ruining the sofa.

Usually comes out

  • everyday dirt, if the code allows;
  • dust and crumbs under the cushions;
  • many fresh stains that have not been rubbed.

May stay

  • stains someone has already tried to clean with an unknown liquid;
  • rings from earlier cleaning;
  • fabric surface damaged by rubbing;
  • wear and faded colour.

Frequently asked questions

Can microfibre be cleaned with water?

The code on the label answers that, not the name of the fabric. W and WS allow water, S and X rule it out. With no code, a test in a hidden spot is needed.

Why do other pages give rules for microfibre, but you do not?

Because a general rule for these fabrics may not fit your sofa. We go by the label.

Do velour rules work for flock?

No. Our velour rules concern viscose pile. They do not automatically suit flock, so here too the label decides.

What if the stain has already been rubbed?

Stop rubbing and do not touch the spot with any new liquid. Write what it was cleaned with and send a photo. That helps a specialist say what can still be done.

How can I tell whether I have microfibre, flock or velour?

You cannot always tell reliably by looking. Send a close-up of the fabric. If the fabric has a pronounced pile, the page velour sofa cleaning is useful too.
