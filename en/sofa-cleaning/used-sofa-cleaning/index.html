What to check before you bring a sofa home

It is much cheaper not to buy the wrong sofa than to clean it. Listing photos do not show smell, so view it in person or ask the seller to answer a few questions. An evasive answer is an answer too.

  • Is the sofa in a flat, or has it stood in a cellar, garage or shed?
  • Does anyone smoke or keep pets in that home?
  • What is on the label, and has it survived at all?
  • Can the cushions be removed so you can look at the seams and gaps?
  • Is there a smell when you lean down to the seat and when you lift a cushion?
  • When was the sofa last cleaned, and with what?

If the sofa has stood in a damp place and smells of mould, think again about buying it: mould can be hard or impossible to remove fully from porous materials, and cleaning does not solve that.

Three things cleaning does not solve

The first is mould in the upholstery. The US Environmental Protection Agency writes that porous materials that have gone mouldy may have to be thrown away. More detail: mould in a sofa.

The second is pests. If you suspect bedbugs or fleas in the sofa, that is a pest control job, not cleaning. The website of the Health Inspectorate (Veselības inspekcija) points to a register of disinfection, insect control and rodent control services. Before that, make sure they really are bedbugs.

The third is the smell of smoking. In studies, smoke residue stayed in cotton and polyester even after 19 months, while carpet cleaning, repainting and wiping surfaces in rooms gave only short-lived results. The smell can usually be reduced, but nobody can promise it will go: cigarette smoke smell in a sofa.

What to do once the sofa is at home

  1. Inspection Take off the cushions, empty the box and look in the gaps. What you find will tell you more about the sofa than the listing did, especially whether people ate on it and what has lived in it.
  2. Airing Before ordering, leave the sofa in a ventilated room, unless it is damp. This helps show what is just the smell of the room.
  3. Photos Send photos of the whole sofa, the worst spot and the label, and write what is known about it.
  4. Price A specialist gets in touch and tells you what to expect and what it costs. We agree the price before the work.

What usually comes out and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • strangers' everyday dirt and greyness;
  • crumbs, dust and hair in the gaps;
  • many stains on the surface.

May stay

  • an old stain of unknown origin;
  • smells of smoking and animals from the upholstery;
  • mould or a mouldy smell;
  • sagging foam and wear.

With a used sofa the origin of the dirt is often unknown, so it is harder to promise anything before the work. The first cleaning often shows what is a stain and what is wear.

Frequently asked questions

Does a used sofa need cleaning straight after buying?

If it looks clean and does not smell, it can wait. If there are stains or a smell, it is sensible to clean it before you start using it. If you suspect pests or mould, deal with that first.

Will cleaning kill bedbugs?

No, cleaning is not pest control. If you see signs, go to a pest control specialist. Very few infestations are solved by a single treatment, writes the US Environmental Protection Agency.

Can it be cleaned at the seller's before I take it home?

That is possible if the sofa is in Riga or the Riga region and the seller agrees. But consider whether you need to view it first. In the form, give the address where the sofa is.
