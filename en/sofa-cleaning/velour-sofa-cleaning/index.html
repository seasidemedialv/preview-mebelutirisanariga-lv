Stain or pile mark: the difference matters

Some of what looks like a stain on velour is not a stain. Velour and chenille are pile fabrics on which pressure marks and shade changes appear with use. A sofa manufacturer describes this as a property of the fabric, not damage. The angle of the light changes how the pile looks, so a seat may seem lighter or darker.

Cleaning removes dirt but does not transform the fabric. If a light patch is only flattened pile, cleaning may not change it. So it helps to photograph the seat from two angles: if the dark area disappears when you change viewpoint, it is most likely not a stain.

What velour needs from cleaning

  • Less moisture. Water and pressure can permanently flatten viscose pile.
  • No rubbing. Viscose pile must not be rubbed or steamed.
  • Blot liquid from the edges towards the centre so that the stain does not spread.
  • Brush the pile only when the fabric is completely dry, and only in the direction of the pile.

Velour can be made from various fibres. The rule we have verified is about viscose pile, so check the fibre content on the label. The rest is set by the code: W, S, WS or X, explained on the page fabric sofa cleaning.

Sources: Brentano Fabrics (a fabric manufacturer) on viscose pile, Long Eaton Sofas (a sofa manufacturer) on marks. Checked 07.11.2026. For velvet and other fibres, the label is what counts.

What to do with a fresh stain on velour

Blot the liquid with a clean, absorbent cloth from the outside in, and do not rub. Use only cool water and do not use heat. Then leave the fabric to dry and do not brush it while it is damp. If the stain is large or the label code is not W, do not wet it again, and send a photo. On different stains: stains on a sofa.

What usually comes out and what may stay on velour

Usually comes out

  • dust in the pile and everyday dirt;
  • fresh drink and food stains, if they have not been rubbed;
  • greyness on seats, if the pile is not damaged.

May stay

  • pressure marks and shade change: that is a property of the fabric;
  • flattened or broken pile;
  • a ring from earlier cleaning that was too wet;
  • a stain that has been rubbed and has already changed the direction of the pile.

If seats have become shiny, that usually means wear, not dirt. In such cases a specialist will tell you before the work whether cleaning makes sense.

How to prepare and what to send

Photograph the seat from the front and from the side, find the label and say whether the stain has already been cleaned. After cleaning, let the fabric dry, and do not sit on it or press it. Ventilate the room as far as the weather allows. We do not promise a drying time, because it depends on air humidity, room temperature and the fabric.

Frequently asked questions

Will velour look like new after cleaning?

We do not promise that. Dirt can usually be reduced, but cleaning does not remove wear, pressure marks or shade differences. Photos from two angles help you judge this before ordering.

Does a velour sofa need special cleaning and a surcharge?

We have no separate velour price; we use fabric sofa prices. Only size, how dirty it is, smell or distance can change the price, and we agree it before the work.

Are velvet and velour the same?

Both are pile fabrics, but the fibre can differ. Our verified rule is about viscose pile, so check the label and fibre content first. That is what determines what may be done.

Why does one seat look darker even though it is not dirty?

The pile there leans in a different direction, and light reflects differently. That is a normal property of velour. Cleaning may not change it.
