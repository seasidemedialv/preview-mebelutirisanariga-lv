How a daybed differs from a sofa

A daybed is usually narrow, often with one raised end and no removable cushions. The upholstery is fixed to the frame, so whatever lands on it stays in one place. A daybed is often used differently from a sofa: in a hallway for putting on shoes, in a bedroom for clothes, or as a dog's favourite spot.

Some daybeds are old, inherited or restored. They often have no care label, as upholstery codes are voluntary. If there is no code, the general advice is to test on a hidden spot first or to ask a specialist. This applies especially to old fabrics whose colour and strength are unknown. If the daybed has a bedding box, empty it before work starts, as the inside is often a different material and may need a different assessment.

What to photograph to get a price

  • The whole daybed from the front, so that the size and shape can be seen.
  • The fabric in close-up, in daylight.
  • Stains and worn areas separately.
  • The label, if there is one under the seat or by the legs.
  • Buttons, seams and decorations, if there are any.
  • The inside, if the daybed has a bedding box.

In the form, also give the approximate length and say whether the daybed is antique or valuable. More on taking photos: how to photograph a sofa to get a price.

What comes out of an old or velour daybed and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and household dirt on the fabric
  • pet hair and surface dirt where the animal lies
  • fresher stains on hard-wearing fabric

May stay

  • old stains of unknown origin
  • a faded shade and wear at the edges
  • pressure marks in velour
  • a smell, if liquid has reached the filling
  • aged, weak fabric that must not be wetted

On velvet and velour: water and pressure can crush the viscose pile for good, so this kind of fabric must not be rubbed. Pressure marks and differences in shade in velour are a property of the fabric. If the fabric is very weak, the honest answer may be that the daybed needs reupholstering rather than cleaning. We do not do reupholstery.

A daybed that a pet sleeps on

If the daybed is a pet's spot, it is important to tell dirt from urine. Hair and surface dirt are a simpler job. Urine is more complicated, because the smell patch can be up to three times larger than the visible stain, and the liquid gets into the foam, where cleaning does not always reach it. If a cat does this repeatedly, it is advisable to see a vet first. More detail: sofa cleaning after a cat or dog.

Frequently asked questions

Can a daybed with buttoned upholstery be cleaned?

Often yes, but buttons and hollows are places where dust collects and where too much moisture should be avoided. Photograph them in close-up, and the specialist will tell you what to expect.

Will you also clean a massage or doctor's couch?

That is a different case, as this kind of couch is used for work. Clinics and practices have a separate page: clinic furniture cleaning.

An inherited daybed smells of age. Will that go away?

Sometimes the smell fades, but we cannot promise that, as it may come from the filling rather than the fabric. If the daybed has stood in a damp attic or basement, check for signs of mould before cleaning and tell the specialist.
