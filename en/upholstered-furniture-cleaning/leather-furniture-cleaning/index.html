First, work out what the material is

In everyday speech the word “leather” covers several different materials, and each needs different care. Before giving a price, the specialist has to know what material you have. If you still have the furniture documents or a label, photograph them, and take a close-up of the surface itself in daylight.

Material What it is What it means
Finished leather leather with a protective coating the surface is cared for with a small amount of water; creams, oils and saddle soap are not meant for it
Suede the sanded underside of leather do not clean stains at home; it has to be assessed separately
Faux leather a film coating on a fabric backing, not leather the fabric furniture price applies; more on the page about faux leather sofa cleaning

What must not be done to leather

  • Do not pour water on leather or soak it with a wet cloth: most leathers suffer from direct contact with water.
  • Do not use saddle soap: it is alkaline.
  • Do not apply creams and oils to finished leather.
  • Do not dry leather with a hairdryer or stand the furniture by a radiator: heat dries leather out, makes it brittle and deforms it.
  • Do not keep leather furniture in a damp room: above 65% relative humidity, mould forms more easily on leather.

For everyday care of finished leather, it is enough to remove the dust and occasionally wipe it with a damp, well-wrung cloth. If a stain is fresh and small, it is often enough to blot it straight away without rubbing. More detail: can a leather sofa be cleaned.

What comes out of leather and what may stay

Usually comes out

  • dust and household dirt on the surface of finished leather
  • darker patches on armrests and headrests, if the dirt has not soaked in
  • fresh stains that have not yet soaked into the leather

May stay

  • a stain that has soaked into the leather, as leather absorbs well
  • pen ink: a complete result on leather cannot be promised
  • cracks, a worn coating and a faded shade, because these are surface damage
  • stains on suede

Cracks and rubbed areas are the most important thing to understand before ordering. Clean leather looks better, but worn areas can become even more visible on a clean surface. Cleaning does not repair this: that is a job for restoration or reupholstering.

Frequently asked questions

Should leather furniture be oiled after cleaning?

Creams and oils are not usually used on finished leather: sources advise wiping and buffing it rather than oiling it. If the furniture has manufacturer's care instructions, follow them, as they apply to your leather specifically.

There is a pen mark on my leather armchair. Can it be removed?

Sometimes it can be reduced, but we cannot promise that, because ink soaks in. Do not rub it or use solvent. Send a close-up photo.

Can a suede armchair be cleaned?

Suede is very sensitive and there is no universal solution for it. Send a photo and the specialist will tell you whether it can be taken on. Do not try to clean stains on suede at home, and if it gets wet, let it dry in the air, away from heat.

Why does mould appear on leather?

Mould forms on leather when the furniture stands in damp air, for example in a cool summer house, and relative humidity is above 65%. The cause of the damp must be dealt with first, otherwise the mould may come back after cleaning.
